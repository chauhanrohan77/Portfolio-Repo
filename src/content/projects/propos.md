## The problem

Dubai's off-plan real estate market has a well-documented pre-sale experience and a poorly served post-handover one. Buyers — many of them remote investors purchasing from outside the UAE — receive a unit and then face a fragmented landscape of tools, portals, and service providers with no single interface connecting them to their asset.

The developer's relationship with the buyer typically ends at handover. DLD registration, RERA dispute processes, EJARI tenancy contracts, rental management, and eventual resale each involve separate platforms, brokers, or government portals. The information asymmetry between developer and owner compounds over time: the developer has the best data on unit quality and building performance, but no incentive to share it once the sale is complete.

PropOS is a product strategy for a boutique developer to close that gap — to extend the owner relationship through the full lifecycle of ownership, and to monetise that relationship across five revenue streams without requiring the developer to operate a consumer tech company.

## The core product decision

**Build the relationship layer, not the transaction layer.** The risk for a boutique developer building a post-handover product is over-investing in features (construction transparency portal, rental yield dashboard, resale marketplace) before establishing the owner relationship that makes those features valuable.

The strategy prioritises relationship capture first: a lightweight owner onboarding flow at handover, direct-to-owner communication, and a single source of truth for building documentation. Monetisable features are sequenced behind that relationship foundation in 12-month increments.

## How it works

The strategy is structured around five revenue streams, sequenced by implementation complexity and owner lifetime value:

1. **Premium services marketplace** — concierge services, maintenance packages, home-fit contractors. Low build cost; captures immediate post-handover demand.
2. **Rental management integration** — referral partnerships with licensed RERA property managers; developer takes a referral fee. Requires no operational investment.
3. **Construction transparency subscription** — live building performance data (energy, common area status, defects resolution) as a premium tier for owners concerned about asset quality.
4. **Captive resale channel** — exclusive resale listings through developer's own brokerage or RERA-licensed partner. High value; requires DLD/RERA licensing alignment.
5. **Data licensing** — anonymised portfolio performance data to institutional investors and REIT operators. Long-tail; requires legal review of DLD data residency requirements.

The 36-month roadmap sequences these streams with go/no-go gates at 12 and 24 months, tied to owner acquisition metrics rather than feature completion milestones.

## Trade-offs & what I'd do next

The strategy assumes the developer has an existing CRM with post-handover owner data and can legally contact owners for product purposes. In practice, DLD data residency rules and owner consent requirements need legal review before any outreach programme is launched.

The captive resale channel is the highest-value stream but the most complex to execute — it requires either a RERA brokerage licence or a formal referral arrangement with a licensed broker, and it only works if the developer can offer owners a genuine pricing advantage over listing publicly.
